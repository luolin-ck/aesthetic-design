### Documentary context and limit

Repeated round-arch/column bays and stone/plaster hierarchy in a documented Early Renaissance building. A partial present-day facade view, not the whole elevation or an unchanged 1419 design.

Perspective is near-frontal but slightly upward; do not measure exact module ratios, column-to-span equality or mathematical proportions from pixels. The image does not show the whole facade, full axial symmetry, plan, vault geometry or interior spatial system. Photograph date is 2018; construction began 1419. Do not label the photograph or every visible addition as 1419. Infant tondi are later additions of 1487 according to the museum; they identify this building but are not a necessary Renaissance style criterion. Upper windows, heraldry and frescoes should not all be attributed to Brunelleschi from this photograph. This is an early Florentine architectural example; the gray-and-white palette and round arches alone cannot classify all media as Renaissance or distinguish later Renaissance revival buildings without provenance.
