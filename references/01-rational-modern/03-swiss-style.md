# Swiss Style: Design Philosophy and Style Guide

- **Name**: International Typographic Style, also Swiss Style
- **Concept type**: A modern graphic-design and typography tradition
- **Core definition**: Make information sequence, hierarchy, and readability the structural framework, using rigorous grids, sans-serif typography, objective photography, and geometry to communicate.

## Attribution Criteria

### 1. Scope and Claims
Assess posters, publications, brand information systems, and digital translations through two separate claims:
- **Historical Swiss Style work**: Postwar work, especially the 1950s–1970s, by designers in Switzerland and related contexts. Establish date, maker, commissions/publications, and museum research; grids or sans-serif type do not prove historical origin.
- **Swiss-style typography**: Assess visible layout and type relationships in contemporary graphics, wayfinding, or websites. A contemporary screenshot cannot establish historical-object identity.

### 2. Core Evidence Dimensions

| Core evidence | How to observe or verify | Why it distinguishes | Limits and alternative explanations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Information roles explain major divisions | Mark reading order of title, date, venue, and text; trace shared axes, column widths, and gaps, checking consistent rules for equivalent information. | The grid carries information logic rather than decorative background squares; unequal blocks remain traceable to shared axes. | A centered three-line card offers little structural evidence. Generic editorial design and UI also use grids. |
| Typography and images work within one system | Check how sizes/weights encode hierarchy and how photographic crops or topic-related geometry align with text axes. | Typographically led asymmetry, controlled type levels, and strict image/text alignment together distinguish more than a font choice. | Photography is optional: type-only and geometric posters can qualify. Actual readability is a quality assessment, not membership eligibility. |
| Rules govern main content rather than a surface patch | Compare series variations accommodating long titles and different content; compare desktop/narrow-screen regrouping. | Establishes that grids and hierarchy organize communication, beyond isolated straight lines or large letters. | A single poster's structure can still be assessed. Missing series evidence leaves continuity unverified, rather than negating the visible style. |

- **Supporting appearances**: Helvetica or other sans-serif type, grayscale with red accents, large voids, and mathematical curves occur elsewhere. No font, color, or photographic subject is a passport to the style.

### 3. Combined Judgment and Neighboring Styles
1. Read actual text first, trace alignment axes, and test whether grid divisions, type levels, and image/text alignment jointly explain and govern the main layout. If so, **Primary style** is supported. Expressive title lettering can carry both the main image and the message, as in Hofmann's wood poster; expressiveness alone is not counterevidence. Conclude **Local borrowing** only when the Swiss-like information rules are confined to a footer or other limited area and do not organize the main image/message.
2. Verify historical identity independently. A partial first-screen crop without complete relationships supports **Insufficient evidence**; list the missing views.
3. Compare [De Stijl](02-de-stijl.md): do divisions encode dates, categories, and reading order, or pursue abstract counterbalance?
4. Compare broader [Modernism](04-modernism.md): a clean sans-serif layout is insufficient without the specific joint grid/type/image system.
5. Random large text, unrelated axes, and filler images counter a primary Swiss-style claim. One deliberate rule break or misalignment is not an automatic veto. Free visual narrative and rigorous grids governing different main areas support **Mixed**, with areas identified.

### 4. Worked Attribution Examples

It is contemporary visual language, not a postwar original without Swiss historical records. |
| Negative, invented | A promotion uses Helvetica, white space, and a red dot, but letters circle around an arc, an isolated product floats centrally, and date/address center independently → **Attribution not supported** overall; similar font and colors only. | Expressive circular text and floating imagery govern the work, not an information grid. ## Position and Background

The [Swiss National Library](https://www.nb.admin.ch/en/the-international-style-1950-1970) identifies the postwar 1950–1970 phase as the International Style, emphasizing mathematical grids, sans-serif type, precise execution, and objective photography replacing personal illustration. It is a specific tradition of posters, publications, and public information, not a name for all modern international commercial design.

## Design Philosophy

1. **Organize logic before expression**: Make title, date, venue, image, and explanation structurally legible without guesswork.
2. **Support variety through shared rules**: Stable references accommodate varying lengths, quantities, and image sizes while enabling rhythmic asymmetry, not rigid symmetric templates.
3. **Remove noninformative noise**: Graphics, color, and typography serve subject and hierarchy. Restraint improves public communication's objectivity and precision without eliminating tension or beauty.

## Design Language

> Digital and brand applications are contemporary translations, not instructions supposedly given by historical posters for today's components.

| Dimension | Typical language | Contemporary translation |
|---|---|---|
| Composition and layout | Mathematical grids, cross-module axes, asymmetric balance, information hierarchy | Derive columns, gutters, and line breaks from real text lengths and image ratios. |
| Color | Often high-contrast grayscale with restrained accents; some bold saturated zones | Mark key actions/categories; never encode a critical state through color alone. |
| Typography | Sans-serif, strong type-scale differences, controlled tracking/leading, left alignment | Choose clear Latin and Chinese sans-serif families; test actual line lengths. |
| Graphics and symbols | Documentary photography, reduced geometry, rhythmic lines, direction signs | Give every image or shape an evidential or directional role, not filler. |
| Materials and texture | Modern printing, paper, faithful image reproduction | Continue precision through crisp rendering, pixel alignment, and stable responsive layouts. |
| Space and interaction | Information coding extends to airport, station, and exhibition wayfinding | Use consistent navigation, explicit form labels, and predictable state feedback. |

## Practical Design Methods

1. List first-glance essentials, second-glance confirmations, and later details before setting heading/body scale.
2. Stress-test grids using the longest real titles, complex schedules, and photographs of varied ratios; uniform Lorem Ipsum hides problems.
3. Limit families and weights, assigning each change a role. Adjust Latin/Chinese baselines and apparent sizes separately.
4. Document axes, crop ratios, and spacing. Complete error, empty, and keyboard-focus states rather than deleting guidance to preserve empty space.

## Design Example

> **Contemporary translation, invented: city public-design forum information system**
> - **Goal**: Help participants find sessions, speakers, and routes in a dense, multivenue forum.
> - **Graphics and web**: Forum name, date, and venue follow a left reading axis; black-and-white speaker portraits and biographies share a right axis. The website reuses type/grid rules, compares times in columns, and groups/collapses schedules by date on mobile.
> - **Wayfinding**: Standees and corridor signs reuse type hierarchy, session numbering, and geometric arrows. One saturated orange marks current sessions and essential registration/check-in, linking rhythm to real actions.

## Representative Cases and Application Boundaries

- **Cases**: Armin Hofmann exhibition posters in the [Swiss National Library feature](https://www.nb.admin.ch/en/the-international-style-1950-1970) show photography, concise type, and geometry balancing within asymmetry. [Letterform Archive's type-history guide](https://letterformarchive.org/news/a-toolkit-for-learning-type-history/) situates Josef Müller-Brockmann and Emil Ruder in the international spread of grids and sans-serif typography.
- **Boundaries**: Strong for academic publishing, transport/exhibition wayfinding, conferences, cultural identities, and dense information tools. Personal handwriting, romantic narrative, and folk textures may coexist with a clear information skeleton; emotional expression need not be prohibited in the name of cold restraint.

## Related Styles and Common Misreadings

- **With [Bauhaus](01-bauhaus.md)**: A comprehensive school spanning buildings, furniture, metal, textiles, and print differs from a focused graphic-communication/typography/grid tradition.
- **With [De Stijl](02-de-stijl.md)**: Shared axes and asymmetry serve information in Swiss Style, rather than making pure abstract relationships the ultimate subject.
- **Misreading**: Helvetica and white space do not replace content hierarchy, cross-module alignment, and precise tracking, leading, and edge relationships.
