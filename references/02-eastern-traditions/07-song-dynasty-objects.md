# Song-dynasty Objects: Design Philosophy and Style Guide

- **Search terms**: Song dynasty ceramics / Song dynasty objects
- **Concept type**: A reference direction grounded in a historical period and specific object types
- **Core definition**: Translate relationships among use, vessel forms for holding/containing, and light-responsive glazes/textures, rather than mechanically applying a single “Song beige” label.

## Attribution Criteria

### 1. Scope and Claims
Song objects constitute a period and material culture, not one dynasty-wide appearance. This guide uses **Ru wine-warming bowls** and **Jian tea bowls** as working samples. Separate:
- **Dating a historical object**: Museum records, archaeology, or professional research must establish Song manufacture. Restrained color cannot date an object.
- **Contemporary translation of a specific branch**: Compare proportions, surfaces, and viewing relationships in objects, brands, exhibitions, and interfaces. Pale beige or an old-bowl photograph does not make the whole system Song-style.
- **Continuity of recipes and firing techniques**: Claims to reproduce a kiln's bodies, glazes, or methods require firing experiments and material tests; renders cannot establish them.

### 2. Core Evidence Dimensions

| Core evidence | How to observe or verify | Why it distinguishes | Limits and alternative explanations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vessel structure and its set/use relationships match a selected sample | Compare rim, wall, foot, and thickness. For a Ru warming bowl, include the ewer used with it to warm wine; for Jian ware, relate the contracted rim and thick walls to whisked-tea use. | Replaces vague antique resemblance with a specific checkable type. | Lobed rims recur elsewhere and cannot date an object. A wine warmer cannot be relabeled a tea bowl without context. |
| Glaze and form jointly organize viewing | Observe color transitions, marking directions, and distribution across curves under different light/angles. In graphics/digital work, inspect how photography and shapes convey form/sheen interaction. | Ru celadon's subtle shifts over ten lobes differ from radial rust markings on black Jian walls, giving more distinction than “elegance.” | Photos/renders can simulate sheen and crackle; they cannot prove recipes, kiln origin, or firing quality. |
| The reference is specific and governs the result | Compare the collection object with the design; identify retained proportions/surface relationships and changed modern functions. Check whether these organize main information in branding/websites. | Gives cross-media translation a clear basis beyond vague restraint or voids. | Displaying a Song ceramic photograph proves subject matter, not the layout's language. Comfort, price, and workmanship do not identify the style. |

- **Supporting appearances**: Beige, celadon gray, black glaze, crackle, petal rims, and voids can belong to other periods or generic minimalism, even in combination. Documented Song objects may also have vivid rust markings or dense impressed/carved decoration; a lack of plainness does not exclude them.

### 3. Combined Judgment and Neighboring Styles
1. For historical dating, prioritize museum research and excavation reports; establish dates before describing form/glaze, rather than projecting a contemporary trend backward.
2. For contemporary attribution, choose a branch such as Ru celadon or Jian blackware. Identify which relationships among proportion, surface, use, and viewing govern the main work. Clear pervasive correspondence supports **Primary style: translation of the selected Ru/Jian language**. Color alone shows only color resemblance; **Local borrowing** requires an identifiable relationship or specific sample correspondence in a limited area. Without a specific branch, list missing full views or references rather than applying a blanket Song label.
3. Compare [Wabi-sabi](12-wabi-sabi.md): does evidence concern a specific historical vessel/glaze system, or irregular hand traces, wear, and impermanence? One crack establishes neither. Song-derived form with a repair/time narrative may be **Mixed** by layer.
4. A smooth beige modern geometric vessel with no specific prototype correspondence does not support Song translation.
5. Missing design notes leave sources unknown; a reliable 2026 production record directly refutes a Song-original claim.

### 4. Worked Attribution Examples

| Case | Input facts → conclusion | Evidence and unresolved questions |
|---|---|---|
| Positive: Ru celadon lotus-shaped warming bowl | National Palace Museum, Taipei records Northern Song Ru manufacture, ten lotus lobes, deep curved walls, continuous celadon, and use with an ewer to warm wine → historical attribution established; the lobe/glaze relationship makes a specific reference sample. | Museum research establishes date/use. Lobes move the walls and glaze catches their turns. Subjective elegance is unnecessary, and this bowl cannot become a standard for all Song objects. |
| Negative, invented | A coffee brand uses beige, cylindrical paper cups, and modern sans-serif type, calling itself Song aesthetics; comparison finds no specific rim/foot or glaze relationship → **Attribution not supported** overall; pale-color resemblance only. | Generic containers and commercial typography govern the work. Comfort and workmanship cannot supply missing correspondence. No further facts are required for this scoped conclusion. |
| Boundary, invented | A 2026 studio bowl closely follows Jian rim/foot proportions and radial brown markings over black glaze, but is sold as an “original Song minimalist tea bowl” → contemporary formal borrowing supported; original-object identity and dynasty-wide minimalism are unsupported. | Modern manufacture refutes ancient-object identity. Undisclosed recipes do not prevent visual comparison. If markings become a small packaging band, that is Local borrowing, not continuity of Jian firing techniques. |

## Position and Background

Northern/southern kilns, everyday/ritual uses, and different users generated diverse objects. The National Palace Museum's [Northern Song Ru lotus warming bowl](https://theme.npm.edu.tw/selection/Article.aspx?sNo=04001032) joins pale celadon and ten-lobed form for wine; its [Southern Song Jian rust-marked tea bowl](https://digitalarchive.npm.gov.tw/Collection/Detail/76858?dep=U) contrasts dark glaze and golden-brown markings for whisked tea. These named samples reveal diversity of shape, color, and surface; they do not summarize all Song visual culture.

## Design Philosophy

> These are contemporary lessons from selected samples, not a common manifesto issued by Song makers or scholars.

1. **Think of form and use as a set**: Containment, warmth, grip, and viewing shape construction. Restore the use context—warmer with ewer, or the tea rim against the mouth—when studying a contour.
2. **Let materials and processes shape appearance**: Glaze thickness, body composition, kiln temperature, and external light determine surface. Color grows through materials rather than an independent swatch applied afterward.
3. **Build detail within restrained forms**: Lobes, continuous walls, flowing marks, and shifting sheen create sustained interest with limited added carving; other Song categories can be richly ornamented.

## Design Language

> Observations and applications concern these samples, not every Song object.

| Dimension | Typical language | Contemporary translation |
|---|---|---|
| Composition and layout | Continuous, considered transitions among rim, body, and foot | Present complete proportions in photography/packaging before drawing viewers into texture. |
| Color | Ru celadon contrasts with Jian black and rust brown | Extract palettes from a named kiln/sample, not a universal pale-beige Song palette. |
| Typography | Vessel shapes prescribe no fixed printed face | Use readable modern body type and a few compact label-like headings. |
| Graphics and symbols | Ten-lobed outlines, radial or flowing kiln markings | Translate a specific contour/texture relation, not a mix of auspicious motifs from every dynasty. |
| Materials and texture | Interdependence of glaze thickness, crackle, sheen, and body | Test physical samples in natural light and touch. Screen textures are atmospheric aids, not ceramic technique. |
| Space and interaction | Size and balance relate to warming wine, whisking, and serving tea | Organize handling, close viewing, and display routes; UI borrows viewing rhythm only. |

## Practical Design Methods

1. Name an exact sample and features, such as the ten-lobed Ru warming bowl and its celadon sheen transitions, instead of untestable “premium Song feeling.”
2. Study contour, surface, and hand actions separately; specify the borrowed layer. Scanning crackle is not the only translation.
3. Build context with accurate photographs, form labels, and material descriptions. Artificially archaic display lettering must not obstruct reading.
4. Test heat-safe grip, stability, cleaning, and color-rendering light; retain clear digital states and feedback rather than weakening usability in pursuit of subtlety.

## Design Example

> **Contemporary translation, invented: museum Song ceramics exhibition and guide**
> - **Goal**: Explain links among form, daily use, and glaze, challenging the idea that Song ceramics are uniformly white.
> - **Graphics and web**: Place the Ru warmer and Jian bowl side by side, accurately labeling ewer/wine and whisked-tea contexts. Offer distinct shape, glaze-detail, and use views for each object. Pair high-contrast modern Chinese serif and sans-serif body type.
> - **Display**: Neutral low-reflection plinths and adjustable light reveal glaze thickness at lobes and metallic Jian markings; avoid bathing the whole room in a uniform dark-beige filter.

## Representative Cases and Application Boundaries

- **Cases**: The [Ru warming bowl](https://theme.npm.edu.tw/selection/Article.aspx?sNo=04001032) unites ten lobes, deep curves, and celadon around wine use; the [Jian tea bowl](https://digitalarchive.npm.gov.tw/Collection/Detail/76858?dep=U) combines contracted rim, thick walls, and radial markings. Follow museum records for exact names, dating, and use.
- **Boundaries**: Useful for vessels, food/tea/wine culture, craft exhibitions, and tactile lifestyle spaces. Ming/Qing furniture and literati landscapes require their own histories; Song aesthetics cannot stand for all traditional Chinese design.

## Related Styles and Common Misreadings

- **With [Literati Ink](08-literati-ink.md)**: Shared historical contexts do not equate three-dimensional vessel/body/glaze/use relationships with painting's brushwork, poetry, and personal expression.
- **With [Wabi-sabi](12-wabi-sabi.md)**: Restraint or crackle does not identify Japanese Wabi-sabi. Discuss specific kiln forms and historical standards separately.
- **Misreadings**: Song objects are not uniformly colorless, undecorated, or minimalist. Verify form, glaze, and historical purpose before borrowing; a Ru wine warmer is not automatically a tea bowl.
