# Classical Design: Design Philosophy and Style Guide

- **Name**: Classical Design
- **Concept type**: Ancient Greek/Roman traditions and their later influence
- **Core definition**: Establish dignity through legible proportions and component relationships across a building, object, or layout, beyond applied capitals and gold borders.

## Attribution Criteria

### 1. Scope and Claims
- **Ancient originals**: Dates, findspots, archaeological sites, and museum records establish historical identity, not appearance alone.
- **Classical language and contemporary borrowing**: Assess references to orders, entablatures, pediments, and proportional hierarchies in buildings, interiors, and graphics. Modern works can show Primary style or Local borrowing without antiquity.
- **Scope**: These dimensions concern Greek/Roman architectural components and translations; no single formula covers all ancient art.

### 2. Core Evidence Dimensions

| Core evidence | How to observe or verify | Why it distinguishes | Limits and alternative explanations |
|---|---|---|---|
| An order forms a component chain rather than an isolated capital | Trace shaft, capital, and entablature—architrave, frieze, cornice—against a specific order diagram. | Specific proportions/connections identify classical language more closely than verticals plus horizontals. | Every work need not contain a complete order; an isolated Corinthian capital establishes only Local borrowing. |
| Bays and component sizes are interdependent | Compare spacing, heights, upper bands, and terminations; identify repetition or shared ratios. | Recognizes module-generated facades rather than arbitrary symmetry. | There is no universal golden-ratio formula. Perspective distorts apparent sizes; prefer elevations/multiple views. |
| Main body, transitions, and top form classical hierarchy | Mark colonnades/pilasters, friezes, cornices, and openings' responses. | Separates an order-organized building from classical parts pasted onto a box. | Roman/later works vary and stack components. Real support versus decorative pilasters requires construction evidence. |
| Graphic translation retains traceable component relationships | Compare borders, friezes, and title zones with a named reference and identify transferred positions/roles. | Makes inspiration checkable rather than classifying all centered layouts as classical. | Posters need no physical support. Centered titles, generic grids, or Chinese serif type are not exclusive evidence. |

- **Supporting appearances**: White stone, symmetry, gold, laurels, isolated capitals, and pediments are leads. Scattered uses support Local borrowing only. Historic paint and dark stone cannot exclude ancient buildings.

### 3. Combined Judgment and Neighboring Styles
1. Verify archaeological/collection records for ancient identity; unknown or modern dates do not prevent visible-form assessment.
2. In buildings, trace order chain, bays, and vertical hierarchy jointly through the facade. In graphics/web, show how specific component relations generate divisions. Corner laurels/capitals on a generic grid support **Local borrowing**, not primary classical organization.
3. Awkward proportions or cheap modern materials concern execution/cost, not automatic cancellation of recognizable language.
4. Compare [Renaissance](15-renaissance.md) and [Neoclassical](18-neoclassical.md): when and in what context was antiquity reorganized? Shared capitals cannot date it. For modern work, begin with broad classical borrowing; subdivide only with specific period modules or Adam/Brunelleschi references.
5. A detail without facade/context means **Insufficient evidence** for whole structure/history. Obtain full views/dates rather than asserting ancient or neoclassical identity.

### 4. Worked Attribution Examples

| Case | Input facts → conclusion | Evidence and unresolved questions |
|---|---|---|
| Positive, invented | A modern entrance elevation has Ionic columns with a continuous entablature, column spacing determining openings, and a terminating cornice → **Primary style: classical borrowing**, with ancient identity excluded. | Component chain, bays, and levels jointly govern. New-build records establish modern date; support versus decoration still requires sections. |
| Negative, invented | A white launch poster has centered type, corner gold laurels, and a generic three-column body, without component ratios/hierarchy/reference → **Attribution not supported** overall; Local borrowing only. | Modern columns govern the layout; colors/symmetry/laurels cannot replace classical relationships. No further investigation is needed for this layout finding. |
| Boundary, invented | A complete facade photo shows columns, entablature, and pediment, but location/date are removed; it might be ancient, Renaissance, or a current hotel → classical vocabulary supported; period has **Insufficient evidence**. | Shared components establish language, not date. Obtain location, construction dates, and records; unknown does not mean ancient or neoclassical. |

## Position and Background

Greek orders are an entry point, beyond a luxury recipe for the entire ancient world. Doric, Ionic, and Corinthian differ in shafts, spacing, and entablature as well as capitals, enabling solidity or slenderness and rich detail. See [EKT: Greek architectural orders](https://www.searchculture.gr/aggregator/portal/thematicCollections/arch_orders?language=en).

## Design Philosophy

1. **Make parts serve the whole**: Every border, capital, and motif should have an explainable role rather than compete independently.
2. **Create expectation through rhythmic repetition**: Bays, columns, and sequences make the next spatial development predictable through continuity.
3. **Differentiate roles and weight**: Support, transition, and termination convey hierarchy without added complexity. These are contemporary interpretive principles, not the sole doctrine of every ancient maker.

## Design Language

> Applications are contemporary suggestions, not ready-made ancient standards.

| Dimension | Typical language | Contemporary translation |
|---|---|---|
| Composition and layout | Axes, bay modules, coordinated part/whole ratios | Establish axis/module first, then controlled local variation. |
| Color | Stone, metal, and period-specific architectural painting | Build stable tonal hierarchy without making white/gold mandatory. |
| Typography | Proportioned stone inscriptions, spacing, and rhythm | Reference Roman inscription proportions for Latin headings; use clear Chinese serif type for dignity. |
| Graphics and symbols | Orders, pediments, fret friezes, narrative relief | Extract one component logic for borders/dividers rather than packing every symbol into a logo. |
| Materials and texture | Stone, limestone, wood, bronze connections | Express weight through joints, thickness, and matte/gloss contrasts rather than superficial veneers. |
| Space and interaction | Distinctive ritual entrances, sequences, and centers | Translate into stable navigation, clear chapters, and consistent component scale. |

## Practical Design Methods

1. Define reliability, public monumentality, or fine elegance before choosing stocky Doric versus slender Ionic/Corinthian references.
2. Test text/color-field structure without motifs. Add friezes only after proportions and spacing establish hierarchy.
3. Retain modular repetition digitally, with direct modern action labels; capitals and foliage are not functional icons.
4. Resolve heights, support, and circulation physically. Decorative pilasters/moldings cannot replace structural or construction-safety decisions.

## Design Example

> **Contemporary translation, invented: annual city-history museum exhibition**
> - **Goal**: Create a credible, dignified, approachable public institution identity.
> - **Graphics/web**: Equal-width object/text modules establish an axis; title above, object center, dates/entry below. A cornice-derived geometric frieze appears only at handbook chapter divisions. Ticketing shares alignment/type with modern high-contrast actions.
> - **Space**: Serial entrance frames stage an inward transition. The system borrows order, proportion, and hierarchy rather than drawing every medium as a Greek temple.

## Representative Cases and Application Boundaries

- **Case**: The Parthenon shows component proportions, materials, and whole-building order. [The Met's ancient Greek architecture study](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/architecture-in-ancient-greece) documents stone, wood, metal connections, and paint.
- **Boundaries**: Useful for museums, memorial/public institutions, academic publishing, and durable trustworthy brands. For lively street/trend identities, borrow proportion control with fewer ritual symbols. One golden-ratio number or white marble cannot judge all works.

## Related Styles and Common Misreadings

- **With [Renaissance](15-renaissance.md)**: Ancient origins differ from fourteenth–seventeenth-century rediscovery through humanism, perspective, and cross-direction modules.
- **With [Neoclassical](18-neoclassical.md)**: Late-eighteenth/early-nineteenth-century archaeology/Enlightenment-based responses to contemporary needs form a distinct movement.
- **Misreading**: Old luxurious European interiors, painted columns, or gold edging do not suffice. Without proportions/hierarchy, many classical signs remain a surface collage.
