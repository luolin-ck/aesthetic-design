# Neoclassical: Design Philosophy and Style Guide

- **Name**: Neoclassicism
- **Concept type**: A European historical art, architecture, and design movement, and contemporary translations
- **Core definition**: Select Greek, Roman, Etruscan, and Pompeian examples through archaeology/documentary study, coordinating walls, furniture, and ornament within clear geometry for contemporary knowledge/life, beyond commercial European-style luxury mixtures.

## Attribution Criteria

### 1. Scope and Claims
- **Historical movement**: Late-eighteenth–early-nineteenth-century European/American architecture, interiors, furniture, vessels, and painting require dates, makers, commissions, and specific ancient references such as Herculaneum/Pompeii publications or Adam drawings.
- **Contemporary translation**: Explicit reference to geometric panels, recomposed antique vessels/friezes, and coordinated scales supports contemporary borrowing, not membership in an eighteenth-century movement.
- **Commercial interiors**: A sofa, plaster molding, and statue are not equivalent to systematic classical reorganization. Criteria principally address architecture/interiors/decorative arts; painting needs composition/subject sources, and UI needs no physical load imitation.

### 2. Core Evidence Dimensions

| Core evidence | How to observe or verify | Why it distinguishes | Limits and alternative explanations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recognizable ancient vocabulary is recomposed | Identify orders, urns, medallions, garlands, sphinxes, and their contour/location relationships. | Replaces vague retro/luxury impressions or generic scrolls with specific references. | Antiquity, Renaissance, and later revivals share components; subdivisions and period documents further distinguish. |
| Geometric/classical divisions constrain ornament | Compare axes, pilasters, rectangle/circle panels, and bands; trace motif placement/scale within frames. | Geometry governs and contains ornament, unlike boundaries dissolved by asymmetric Rococo curves. | Neoclassicism is not undecorated. Adam interiors can be intricate and colorful while retaining geometric control. |
| Parts vary within one vocabulary and scale system | Compare ceiling, walls, carpet, furniture, or cover/chapter/body frames for coordinated divisions/motif family. | Establishes whole-system reinterpretation beyond one Venus statue. | A matching suite may qualify only locally; coordinated colors are not exclusive evidence. |
| Ancient examples transform into period/current use | Compare sketches, books, accounts, and prototypes; explain adaptations of temples/tombs/frescoes to rooms, halls, or media. | Distinguishes redesign from literal replica, random furniture mixtures, or retro slogans. | A designer label is a lead. Without process records, visible form may support broad classical/neoclassical borrowing, not invented archaeological motives. |

- **Supporting appearances**: Black/white/gold, plaster frames, busts, Roman numerals, polished marble, velvet, and crystals can be local collages without coordinated divisions. Expense, solid wood, or real stone are not attribution requirements.

### 3. Combined Judgment and Neighboring Styles
1. Separate historical movement from contemporary form. Verify dates/documents for history; for form, trace specific vocabulary, containing geometry, and cross-part variation. A particular neoclassical model such as Adam's governing the whole supports **Primary style**.
2. A statue/lamp in otherwise unrelated minimal furniture/walls supports **Local borrowing**. Centered type and thin frames without identifiable components/transformations give **Insufficient evidence** for narrowing generic typography to Neoclassicism.
3. Compare [Rococo](17-rococo.md): boundaries generated by C/S rocaille versus geometry containing ornament. Transitional panels with internal asymmetric curls are **Mixed**, not necessarily pure examples.
4. Compare [Classical](13-classical.md) and [Renaissance](15-renaissance.md): shared orders/pediments differ from new eighteenth-century archaeological panels, medallions, and object designs integrated into homes, libraries, and institutions. Without dates or distinctive reorganization, retain broad classical language and leave period unverified.

### 4. Worked Attribution Examples

| Case | Input facts → conclusion | Evidence and unresolved questions |
|---|---|---|
| Positive, invented | A contemporary chamber hall explicitly compares Adam drawings; pilasters/panels contain urns/friezes; ceiling coffers and seat details vary the same motifs, with traceable transformations → **Primary style: contemporary neoclassical borrowing**. | Geometric division, decorative placement, and cross-part variation establish the system, beyond neatness. New construction is not an eighteenth-century original. |
| Negative, invented | A minimal apartment has a modular sofa, David plaster head, and gold floor lamp, with no coordinated classical divisions/motifs → **Attribution not supported** overall; Local borrowing of objects. | Furniture plus a head does not create a classical system. Existing spatial evidence supports modern minimal mixing without further investigation. |
| Boundary, invented | An undated European interior has geometric classical panels/pilasters containing asymmetric shell/S-shaped leaves → **Mixed** classical framework and Rococo surfaces; historical Neoclassicism is not yet established. | Outer/inner rules differ and resemble transitional practice. Obtain place/date/designer records. Later historical confirmation should retain the observed mixed form. |

## Position and Background

This guide separates the late-eighteenth–early-nineteenth-century movement from today's broad interior marketing labels. Herculaneum/Pompeii excavations, the Grand Tour, and archaeological books/engravings refreshed understandings of Greek, Roman, and Etruscan cultures; see [The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/neoclassicism). National/design responses range from monumental public architecture to bright, intricate coordinated interiors, without one decorative density.

## Design Philosophy

1. **Ground authority in documented examples**: Select and refine ruins, frescoes, reliefs, and vessels through research rather than mechanically copying outlines.
2. **Coordinate each part within the whole**: Ceiling, panels, mantels, carpet, side tables, and silver share geometric rhythm/motif scales rather than an unrelated assortment.
3. **Rework history for current life**: Ancient temples/baths become living, reading, and public spaces rather than merely looking excavated. [V&A's Robert Adam account](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/robert-adam-neoclassical-architect-and-designer) exemplifies transformation and whole-room coordination.

## Design Language

> Contemporary brand/publishing/digital applications are not universal historical color, type, or UI rules.

| Dimension | Typical language | Contemporary translation |
|---|---|---|
| Composition and layout | Axes, rectangular/circular/octagonal panels, controlled repetition | Set shared title/image/caption positions and geometric containers. |
| Color | Varied by maker/object: Adam's pale green/lilac/terracotta/blue with white/gold; jasperware relief contrasts | Choose a documented example rather than mandatory black/white/gold or beige. |
| Typography | Roman inscriptions and late-eighteenth-century high-contrast, vertical-axis faces such as Bodoni/Didot | Use clear high-contrast serif short headings; readable Chinese serif/sans-serif body families. |
| Graphics and symbols | Frets/leaves, urns, medallions, garlands, sphinxes, fans | Limit families/line scales; contain them in borders or chapter badges. |
| Materials and texture | Marble, painted stucco, wood inlay, gilt bronze, jasperware | Match texture/precision emphasis to functional hierarchy. |
| Space and interaction | Total design coordinates ceiling/wall/floor/furniture | Translate into consistent components/spacing rather than fake plaster everywhere. |

## Practical Design Methods

1. Specify Adam panels, Wedgwood reliefs, or French revolutionary geometry rather than merging Greek, Renaissance, Baroque, and Rococo into European style.
2. Establish reusable margins, columns, fine frames, and corner scales; test real short/long text.
3. Use friezes for hierarchy/closure at covers, chapter starts, or key quotations, with clear modern information underneath.
4. Check shared proportions physically across ceiling, panels, furniture, and lights. Digitally, preserve navigation/card/type consistency without sacrificing reading or operation to retro ornament.

## Design Example

> **Contemporary translation, invented: city chamber ensemble season booklet and booking**
> - **Goal**: Communicate scholarship and rigorous programming with fluent modern visitor information.
> - **Print**: Fine double rectangular lines divide program, performer, and background notes into fixed modules. An Adam-derived leaf band appears only at seasonal chapter openings. Gray-green and warm white follow ensemble identity.
> - **Digital**: Booking shares type, contrast, and geometry. Seat maps, prices, and date filters remain obvious modern controls; classical language marks identity/chapters without hiding dates, places, or actions.

## Representative Cases and Application Boundaries

- **Cases**: V&A's Adam drawings for Syon House's Long Gallery/library and *Works in Architecture of Robert and James Adam* show Roman frescoes, orders, reliefs, and panels recomposed into coordinated life settings. See [V&A](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/robert-adam-neoclassical-architect-and-designer).
- **Boundaries**: Useful for halls, museums, libraries, scholarly/legal publishing, hotels, and historically grounded brands. Fast-changing online/street work may retain geometry with fewer antique details. Rich relief/paint is compatible; historical Neoclassicism is not empty cold minimalism.

## Related Styles and Common Misreadings

- **With [Classical](13-classical.md)**: Antiquity is the source; later archaeology/publishing enables selective transformation and coordinated interiors.
- **With [Rococo](17-rococo.md)**: Free asymmetric curls differ from containing geometry/axes/documented friezes, with common transitional mixtures.
- **Misreading**: Modern sofa, plaster frame, and Venus cannot replace archaeological transformation and whole-space proportional coordination.
