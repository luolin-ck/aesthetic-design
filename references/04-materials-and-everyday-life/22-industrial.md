# Industrial Interiors: Design Philosophy and Style Guide

- **English name**: Industrial interior design
- **Concept type**: Contemporary spatial label inspired by industrial buildings and adaptive reuse
- **Core definition**: Understanding existing structural spans, services, and material textures and making them participate honestly in new everyday or public uses, rather than constructing a dirty, dilapidated imitation factory.

---

## Attribution Criteria

### 1. Scope and Claims
Industrial here is primarily a contemporary interior/spatial visual label, not the academic or manufacturing discipline of Industrial Design. Separate three claims:
- **Industrial heritage and factory-reuse identity**: Establish a building's former factory, warehouse, or power-station use through construction dates, use histories, and conversion archives. An authentic factory converted into a white-box gallery with every beam and duct concealed retains its reuse identity, but its present visible interior is not thereby industrial in style.
- **Actual load-bearing structures and functioning services**: Visible beams, brickwork, or ducts do not prove structural or service functions. Check construction drawings and material schedules.
- **Contemporary industrial visual language**: Examine how column/beam networks, exposed services and fasteners, brick/concrete/metal boundaries, and furnishings organize the main space. Decorative frames and imitation brick in a new building can support visual attribution when these relationships remain coherent.

For graphic and digital work, examine borrowed framework divisions, numbering, or technical-diagram language; do not impose factory-scale architectural conditions.

### 2. Core Evidence Dimensions

| Core evidence dimension | How to observe or verify | Why it discriminates | Limits and alternative explanations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **An industrial framing network directly articulates space** | Mark columns, beams, trusses, and large metal window grids—or decorative equivalents—in full views; examine how repeated bays frame functions. | Grounds attribution in an industrial architectural skeleton rather than a brick-print wall. | Small homes need not have factory spans. Visible steel does not prove load-bearing function; verify engineering separately. |
| **Services, joints, and fabrication language continue through principal areas** | Trace ducts/conduits, tray connections, metal frames, exposed bolts, and suspended-light brackets, rather than count isolated fittings. | Distinguishes systematic industrial construction vocabulary from dark modern or rustic reclaimed-wood furniture. | Pipes and valves may be decorative. This neither cancels visual attribution nor proves functioning infrastructure. |
| **Brick, concrete, and metal surfaces correspond to component/interface roles** | Compare boundaries with walls, columns, partition frames, and worktops; check continuity across viewpoints. | Connects texture and framing into one spatial language rather than scattered distressed finishes. | Veneer brick, cement paint, and printed laminates can support visual borrowing. Authenticity, price, and maintenance need separate assessment. |
| **Principal furniture and lighting share the industrial reference** | Check whether workbench frames, metal partitions, and factory shades extend frame joints or workshop-object vocabulary. | Supports a primary spatial style rather than assigning the entire room a label from one iron lamp. | Fabric and upholstery are allowed. Habitability and comfort concern quality, not style membership. |

- **Common supporting appearances**: Brick stickers, black pipe shelves, Edison bulbs, rusty gear ornaments, and yellow/black stripes. A few such props give **insufficient evidence of an overall industrial interior**. Bright glass, pale terrazzo, or warm wood does not exclude reused factories with clearly retained column grids and service order; darkness and decay are not requirements.

### 3. Combined Judgment and Neighboring Styles
1. **Verify continuity among framing/joints, material boundaries, and principal furnishings**:
   - If industrial framing and fabrication relationships organize the main space, conclude **Primary style: Industrial**. In small spaces, continuous metal partitions, ceiling services, and furniture can establish this without authentic factory spans.
   - One brick wall, black accents, or a tabletop gear in a room governed by classical scroll moldings and chandeliers is **Local borrowing**.
   - Cropped detail views require **Insufficient evidence of the overall style**. Do not conflate classification with rough construction, hazards, or imitation materials.
2. **Challenge neighboring explanations**:
   - Compare **[Modernism](../01-rational-modern/04-modernism.md)**: Both use steel, glass, and concrete. Is this generic, clean modern geometry, or specifically translated workshop bays, exposed services, rivets/bolts, and workshop objects? Historical overlaps do not establish legally recognized industrial-heritage status for either visual language.

| **Counterexample (hypothetical)** | A reception room governed by classical carved furniture, velvet curtains, and crystal chandeliers adds brick-print wallpaper behind a television and one rusty gear → **Local borrowing of industrial symbols; overall industrial attribution is not supported**. | **Evidence**: Main boundaries, furniture contours, and lighting lack a continuing industrial frame/joint vocabulary. **Outstanding**: Brick authenticity need not be checked to identify its local decorative role. |

Even if all interiors are white boxes, that limits the style claim without changing documented reuse identity. |

## Position and Background

Industrial interior design is a label used in interiors, commercial spaces, and urban regeneration; distinguish it from product/engineering Industrial Design. Architectural Digest traces it through factory and warehouse conversions, drawing on named practitioners and emphasizing exposed components, services, and material detail. It has no common manifesto or fixed decoration formula. See [Architectural Digest: Industrial Interior Design: Everything You Need to Know](https://www.architecturaldigest.com/story/industrial-interior-design-101).

---

## Design Philosophy

> These practical principles are distilled from industrial conversions and contemporary spatial references.

1. **Understand what already exists**: Column spans, clerestory daylight, and traces of former use are conditions to understand and employ, not merely nostalgic backdrops.
2. **Activate old materials through new uses**: Retain brickwork, concrete columns, or crane beams for specific zoning or circulation purposes. Roughness does not justify preserving leaks, loose surfaces, or poor acoustics.
3. **Make the new/old relationship legible**: Clean new restroom modules, lighting tracks, or acoustic panels can converse with an older frame. Users should understand the spatial logic; every new item need not be distressed.

---

## Design Language

> These are contemporary translations. Qualified architects and engineers must resolve actual structural alterations and building services to applicable requirements.

| Design dimension | Typical characteristics | Contemporary application |
|---|---|---|
| **Composition and layout** | Repeated bays, truss lines, large divided windows, generous horizontal/vertical scales. | Use clear columns and grids for print/web, preserving large-image presence rather than fragmenting it. |
| **Color** | Brick, iron, galvanized steel, plain concrete, and later coatings form grounded palettes. | Establish lightness levels and use visible safety yellow or signal orange for key actions. |
| **Typography** | Equipment plates, freight stencils, and zone-numbering systems. | Keep Chinese body text readable in sans serif; use stencil forms sparingly for context-specific short titles or zone IDs. |
| **Graphics and symbols** | Structural grids, dimension lines, flow arrows, technical schedules. | Make lines and IDs explain structure, circulation, or parameters; avoid meaningless fake engineering drawings. |
| **Materials and texture** | Old/new seams, welds/bolts, brushed or hot-rolled metals. | Choose durable, cleanable finishes; loose parts, flaking rust, and sharp edges do not express desirable roughness. |
| **Space and interaction** | Original industrial scales meet new public, work, and domestic scales. | Use distinct modular boundaries and direct feedback online; rotating gears are not mandatory entry screens. |

---

## Practical Design Methods

1. **Assess structure, acoustics, and thermal performance first**: Identify valuable spans, daylight, and traces in factories or unfinished spaces; plan insulation, absorption, and accessible circulation alongside new functions.
2. **Derive branding from actual making or engineering**: Use real processes, part numbers, and material qualities for manufacturers or workshops. Do not attach false bolts, hazard stripes, or fabricated inspection stamps to unrelated dessert shops or goods.
3. **Organize signage through numbering and line weights**: Stable grids, clear separators, and genuine material close-ups can create industrial recognition while preserving readable body information; avoid full-page distressed filters.
4. **Maintain engineering safety and upkeep**: Digital modules may have direct rectangular divisions and parameter feedback. Physical exposed surfaces/services still require dust sealing, safe edges, and electrical protection; style is no excuse to omit professional treatment.

## Design Example

> **Contemporary translation (hypothetical): a public repair workshop in a reused warehouse**
> - **Goal**: Reveal repair processes for household objects and small appliances, making participation approachable.
> - **Space and signage**: Retain a historically legible load-bearing brick wall and steel roof after reinforcement and dust sealing. Add clean modular birch-plywood reception, tool lending, and quiet work areas with exposed galvanized conduits along clear circulation.
> - **Brand and website**: Reuse actual shelf IDs and repair-process cards; paper repair slips use clear, dirt-tolerant tables. Booking proceeds through object category, fault description, and available bench slots without requiring residents to understand specialist jargon. The old frame contains efficient public service rather than concealing disorganization.

## Representative Cases and Application Boundaries

- **Representative case**: Herzog & de Meuron's Tate Modern conversion in London transforms Bankside Power Station's turbine-hall scale and brick exterior into new exhibition/public sequences. It is a reference for industrial adaptive-reuse strategies, not a claim that the architects simply defined the whole project as industrial interior decoration. See [Herzog & de Meuron: 126 Tate Modern](https://www.herzogdemeuron.com/projects/126-tate-modern/).
- **Application boundaries**: Useful for urban reuse, creative offices, workshops, coffee roasting, and engineering/manufacturing brands. Low-ceiling homes and phone screens can borrow structural clarity and modules without imitating factory heaviness. Exposed materials must not conceal poor construction or maintenance.

## Related Styles and Common Misreadings

- **Related styles**:
  - **Industrial vs [Modernism](../01-rational-modern/04-modernism.md)**: Modernism spans architecture, objects, and communication; industrial interiors are a contemporary label for translating factory framing, services, and textures. They occupy different conceptual levels.
  - **Industrial vs [Web Brutalism](../06-digital-and-futures/36-web-brutalism.md)**: Web Brutalism examines raw HTML organization and experiments against commercial design conventions in hypertext, a different medium from industrial interiors.
- **Common misreading**: Painting everything black, dimming lights, adding fake brick and rusty metal—or confusing interior style with Industrial Design as a product discipline.
