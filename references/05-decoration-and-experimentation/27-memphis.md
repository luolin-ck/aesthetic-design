# Memphis: Design Philosophy and Style Guide

- **English name**: Memphis design
- **Concept type**: Design group and the postmodern influence of its works
- **Core definition**: Furniture and objects become emotional and symbolic characters through unconventional combinations of shapes, colors, and patterns that invite new ways of looking.

---

## Attribution Criteria

### 1. Scope and Claims
Separate three claims:
- **Historical group work**: Originals by the 1980s Italian Memphis Group, made or issued by the group, require authorship, maker, date, and collection records.
- **Memphis visual language**: Compare observable component and surface relationships in graphics, furniture, spaces, or interfaces with Carlton or the Super lamp. This neither requires the maker's reference list nor confers historical group identity.
- **Actual influence or rebellious intention**: Claims of inspiration from a member or a particular anti-functionalist declaration require interviews or documents, not appearance.

### 2. Core Evidence Dimensions

| Core evidence dimension | How to observe or verify | Why it discriminates | Limits and alternative explanations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Basic geometry forms an unconventional main silhouette or component composition** | Mark supports, component scales, tilts, overlaps and overhangs, and compare how they interact with contrasting surfaces. Such relationships can occur within an approximately bilateral frame, as in Carlton. | Turns circles/squares/triangles into theatrical characters rather than ordinary flat geometry. | Overall asymmetry, eccentric support and overhangs are possibilities, not required features. Objects need not wobble or be unusable. |
| **Components retain distinct color, pattern, or shape identities** | Compare adjoining color fields, directions, and material appearances; check emphasized contrast at joints. | Preserves assemblage and character rather than unified monochrome modern geometry. | Every component need not carry a different pattern; photos cannot establish laminate versus wood. |
| **Patterns collide actively with volumes or graphic hierarchies** | Trace dots, waves, and irregular repeats across particular surfaces, boundaries, and solid colors. | Shows pattern participating in form rather than random background speckles. | Strong component offsets may support unprinted works; one bacterial pattern or dot is not mandatory. |
| **Unconventional relationships recur through the main object or series** | Compare contours, secondary parts, and extensions for continuing variations in offsets and pattern collisions. | Establishes primary organization beyond a playful corner sticker. | Organization strength is not taste or workmanship; rough or messy examples may still qualify. |

- **Common supporting appearances**: Saturated contrasts, elementary geometry, colored furniture, dots, and waves. Each alone offers **insufficient evidence of Memphis**; examine their unconventional component, scale and surface relationships together. Overall symmetry does not exclude this language.

### 3. Combined Judgment and Neighboring Styles
1. **Judgment sequence**:
   - Begin with the component/block composition and its scale contrasts, angled parts or unexpected combinations. Examine how colors/patterns reinforce the juxtaposition. A broadly symmetric frame can support these relationships; do not require eccentric support.
   - If contour and surface relationships jointly govern the work, conclude **Primary style: Memphis visual language**. A small dotted background/header patch is **Local borrowing**.
2. **Challenge neighboring explanations**:
   - Compare **[Bauhaus](../01-rational-modern/01-bauhaus.md)**: Shared primary colors or geometry are insufficient. Are forms reinforcing offsets, scale changes, and pattern collisions, or organized through common modules, aligned grids, and functional components? Historical Bauhaus identity still requires archives.
   - Compare **[Pop Art](28-pop-art.md)**: Packaging, celebrity imagery, or advertising extraction/reconstruction may independently support Pop. If component assemblage and media-image reworking both govern, conclude **Mixed** borrowing.
   - **Conflicts and gaps**: A cropped pattern cannot establish a whole object/space; use **Insufficient evidence**. Missing documents limit historical group identity, not observable language.

### 4. Worked Attribution Examples

| Example type | Input facts → conclusion | Evidence and outstanding questions |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive example (hypothetical)** | A display shelf uses a tilted triangular support under an off-center cylinder and a strongly overhanging top; parts carry saturated colors, dots, and waves. Posters repeat the offset overlaps → **Primary style: Memphis visual language**. | **Evidence**: Eccentric contour, differentiated components, and surface collisions govern physical and graphic work. **Outstanding**: Loads and stability require engineering tests. |
| **Counterexample (hypothetical)** | A teaching poster aligns red circles, yellow triangles, and blue squares centrally in equal grid cells without disproportion, crossing patterns, or collisions → **Attribution not supported as primary Memphis**. | **Evidence**: The shapes create ordinary regular geometry without offset tension; they cannot authenticate Bauhaus originals either. **Outstanding**: None. |
| **Boundary example (hypothetical)** | A store website has a regular product grid and standard checkout, with tilted cylinders/wavy triangles only in a header; the brand's printed poster lets those forms occupy the full image → **Local borrowing on the website; Primary style borrowing on the poster**. | **Evidence**: Dominance differs by medium; the poster's conclusion cannot classify the full website. **Outstanding**: Actual references remain unknown; these are not historical group works. |

## Position and Background

Memphis is closely associated with early-1980s Italian postmodern design. Designers around Ettore Sottsass combined saturated color, patterns, exaggerated geometry, and materials of different cultural status, making everyday objects expressive statements. [V&A: What is Postmodernism?](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/what-is-postmodernism) discusses rebellion against modernist dogma, complexity, and commercial mass communication. Memphis is representative of postmodern design, not synonymous with all of it.

---

## Design Philosophy

1. **Let daily objects express feelings and attitudes**: Alongside physical functions, objects can embody humor, drama, and character associations that address emotional life.
2. **Use unconventional combinations for tension**: Patterns, contrasting fields, and geometry may break coaxial symmetry and collide, while retaining a recognizable logic.
3. **Challenge material and taste hierarchies**: Inexpensive fast-food-table plastic laminate can share center stage with wood and metal, bringing surface decoration and symbols into design value.

---

## Design Language

> These contemporary applications do not imply one fixed color or component formula across all historical group work.

| Design dimension | Typical characteristics | Contemporary application |
|---|---|---|
| **Composition and layout** | Eccentric supports, tilts, offset stacks, sculptural masses. | Establish a stable visual center, then add a few tilts or boundary crossings. |
| **Color** | Lemon yellow, pale blue, vivid red, and patterned grounds in strong juxtaposition. | Assign main, supporting, and accent areas while permitting local clashes. |
| **Typography** | Graphic translations include heavy geometric/module-based or offset display type. | Put personality in headlines; keep body and functional labels consistent. |
| **Graphics and symbols** | Circles, squares, triangles, cylinders, dots, zigzags, irregular repeats. | Build an original pattern library with adjustable density; avoid unrelated overload. |
| **Materials and texture** | Printed laminate, painted wood, tubing, and glass in contrast. | Use seams, solid fields, and prints to strengthen each component's character. |
| **Space and interaction** | Furniture as stage characters; theatrical arrangements. | Create distinctive exhibition nodes; limit playful digital imagery locally and retain stable task flows. |

---

## Practical Design Methods

1. **Choose one principal silhouette and two supporting forms**: Use black/white silhouettes before patterns to settle proportions rather than let every piece demand attention.
2. **Link media through few repeated motifs**: Carry two or three core shapes/patterns across posters, tickets, and displays, keeping brand recognition beneath collisions.
3. **Break local alignment, preserve information groups**: Headlines and images may cross color fields; dates, places, and prices must remain grouped/readable. Stress-test with actual long and short text.
4. **Verify safety and intuitive use first**: Check loads, corners, circulation, button states, navigation, and forms before theatrical surfaces.

## Design Example

> **Contemporary translation (hypothetical): an independent publishing and art-book fair**
> - **Goal**: Express diverse creative voices and lively exchange.
> - **Print**: Tilted intersecting bookshelf forms lead the image. Dots, zigzags, and diagonals distinguish exhibitions, author talks, and independent stalls. Headlines cross local fields; dates and venue remain in a plain lower-right safe area.
> - **Space and website**: Quickly assembled colored-laminate displays echo the forms. Standard exhibition cards and registration buttons support the ticket site, concentrating visual conflict in the first banner.

## Representative Cases and Application Boundaries

- **Representative cases**: Ettore Sottsass's 1981 [Carlton Room Divider at MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/292736), made by Memphis in wood and plastic laminate, combines horizontal and sloped storage/division components into an anthropomorphic silhouette. Martine Bedin's wheeled Super lamp in [V&A's postmodernism overview](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/what-is-postmodernism) likewise gives an everyday object character.
- **Application boundaries**: Useful for youth culture, experimental consumer brands, pop-ups, and expressive furniture. Limit clashes in sustained-focus office software, complex healthcare/finance dashboards, and long reading. Memphis humor and drama are not confined to children's subjects.

---

## Related Styles and Common Misreadings

- **Related styles**:
  - **Memphis vs [Bauhaus](../01-rational-modern/01-bauhaus.md)**: Examine what geometry does, not whether both use basic shapes/colors. Bauhaus explored form/material/industrial production relationships; Memphis used offsets, disproportion, and laminate surfaces to challenge functionalist dogma.
  - **Memphis vs [Pop Art](28-pop-art.md)**: Pop extracts and repeats packaging, celebrities, and commercial symbols; Memphis more often begins with three-dimensional component combinations, surfaces, and geometric character.
- **Common misreading**: Filling an image with colored dots, waves, and triangles. A useful design can identify its principal component, repeated patterns, and the particular offset that challenges expectation.
