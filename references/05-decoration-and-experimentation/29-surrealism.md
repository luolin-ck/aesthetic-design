# Surrealism: Design Philosophy and Style Guide

- **English name**: Surrealism
- **Concept type**: Early-twentieth-century art/literary movement and subsequent visual methods
- **Core definition**: Altering familiar everyday relationships to interrupt rational conventions and open associations involving dreams, desires, and the subconscious.

---

## Attribution Criteria

### 1. Scope and Claims
Separate three claims:
- **Historical movement original**: Art/literature by movement members after André Breton's 1920s manifesto requires date, authorship, exhibition, and scholarly records.
- **Surreal visual language: figurative displacement route**: Compare observable disruptions of familiar experience in contemporary images, objects, or exhibitions with historical examples such as *Object*. These criteria address figurative relationships, not historical membership or every abstract/automatist practice.
- **Automatic process or subconscious psychology**: Claims of unconscious automatic drawing, actual dreams, or the author's subconscious require process records or author documents. Never infer psychology from appearance alone.

### 2. Core Evidence Dimensions

| Core evidence dimension | How to observe or verify | Why it discriminates | Limits and alternative explanations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Recognizable everyday objects, settings, or perceptual expectations exist** | State normal scale, materials, position, or use, then compare the visible facts. | Supplies a checkable reference for estrangement rather than a vague strange-looking judgment. | Photorealism is unnecessary; drawings, simple graphics, and physical objects can work. Pure abstraction requires another route, such as process evidence for automatism. |
| **A key ordinary relationship is displaced, replaced, or put in conflict** | Identify collisions between drinking and touch, body and scale, inside/outside, or gravity/support. | Locates the effect in relational mismatch rather than merely displaying a rare odd-shaped object. | Fantasy, film effects, and metaphors can share methods. Impossibility alone does not establish historical Surrealism. |
| **Displacement forms sustained visual connections and associations** | Trace transformations, juxtapositions, repeats, or grafted scenes linking previously separate experiences. | Distinguishes governing expression from a single retouching error or malfunction. | No unique solution or profound meaning is required. Open meanings and rough execution may still qualify. |
| **Experiential conflict occupies the principal object or viewing process** | Compare subject, background, and series extensions for central versus peripheral displacement. | Separates primary borrowing from small decorative effects. | Realistic light can clarify relationships but is not mandatory; nonrealistic or abstract branches cannot be excluded for lacking it. |

- **Common supporting appearances**: Odd colors, floating objects, AI distortions, vague dreaminess. None alone establishes Surrealism. Known wide-angle distortion or ordinary suspension such as hanging balloons counters the visual claim **only when no further disruption of everyday experience is organized**. Real props and staging do not themselves refute surreal relationships; missing background information is not counterevidence.

### 3. Combined Judgment and Neighboring Styles
1. **Judgment sequence**:
   - State the everyday expectation, identify the changed material/scale/position/function relationship, and trace how the work develops its tension.
   - If the displacement governs viewing, conclude **Primary style: Surreal visual language**. One altered secondary icon is **Local borrowing**.
2. **Challenge neighboring explanations**:
   - Compare **ordinary Fantasy**: Is the image showing a creature native to an invented world, such as a dragon/enchanted forest, or making familiar experiences collide within one object? They can overlap; a single image does not prove mutual exclusion.
   - Compare **[Pop Art](28-pop-art.md)**: Experiential displacement and association differ from consumer/media extraction, copying, and circulation grammar. Both may support **Mixed** borrowing.
   - **Conflicts and gaps**: A static image without processes/archives requires separate **Insufficient evidence** findings for abstract automatism, the author's psychology, unseen exhibition areas, or historical membership claims.

### 4. Worked Attribution Examples

| Example type | Input facts → conclusion | Evidence and outstanding questions |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive example (hypothetical)** | An exhibition poster shows normally proportioned cup, saucer, and spoon covered in long hair. The cup still implies drinking; related images show a hand approaching the furry cup → **Primary style: Surreal visual language**. | **Evidence**: Drinking expectation and animal-fur touch form a precise conflict governing the series. **Outstanding**: Do not infer the designer's subconscious dream or call these contemporary hypothetical posters the 1936 original. |
| **Counterexample (hypothetical)** | An ultra-wide room photograph slightly bends edge doorframes; party balloons hang from fine threads. Furniture scales, contact surfaces, and uses otherwise remain ordinary → **Attribution not supported as primary Surrealism**. | **Evidence**: Known lens distortion and normal suspension do not develop further experiential displacement. **Outstanding**: None. |
| **Boundary example (hypothetical)** | A fully abstract line drawing has only an unsourced caption saying it was drawn automatically. A website uses it above an ordinary article list → **Insufficient evidence under the figurative route; do not exclude broad Surrealism. The website's overall interaction is not surreal on this basis**. | **Evidence**: Abstract work needs an automatist process route, and one cover illustration does not classify site interaction. **Outstanding**: Verify actual automatic methods and historical connections through process/document records. |

## Position and Background

Surrealism is associated with André Breton's 1924 manifesto and explorations of dreams, the subconscious, and freedom from rational aesthetic convention. It includes realistically rendered absurd juxtapositions and abstract automatic writing/drawing. See [MoMA: Tapping the Subconscious: Automatism and Dreams](https://www.moma.org/momaorg/shared/flattened_pages/moma_learning/themes/surrealism/tapping-the-subconscious-automatism-and-dreams/). Contemporary communication, exhibitions, and commerce often translate its estrangement/displacement of ordinary figurative objects.

---

## Design Philosophy

1. **Create precise deviations from clear experience**: Recognizable objects, scales, and textures make one changed relationship perceptually arresting and invite reinterpretation.
2. **Use unexpected juxtaposition to stimulate association**: Emotion, puns, and cross-sensory links can connect unrelated objects poetically. Physical laws need not apply, but the combination should serve the project's narrative.
3. **Keep interpretation open**: Multiple sensory/emotional reactions can coexist. Avoid fixing every element into one didactic symbol while maintaining an emotional through-line.

---

## Design Language

> These contemporary interface/exhibition suggestions are not inherent rules of the historical movement.

| Design dimension | Typical characteristics | Contemporary application |
|---|---|---|
| **Composition and layout** | Context shifts, scale changes, floating, inside/outside grafts. | Build a believable ordinary baseline, then dramatically alter one relationship. |
| **Color** | From calm realistic light to dreamlike or high contrast. | Keep environmental light/tone coherent so the altered relationship stands out. |
| **Typography** | Humorous/absurd gaps among words, images, and contexts. | Let a short title participate in puns; keep exhibition/product explanations clear. |
| **Graphics and symbols** | Dreams, ordinary objects, body fragments, transformations, unexpected grafts. | Organize a main image around one rule such as material replacement or reversed scale. |
| **Materials and texture** | Sensory conflict between an object's use and its surface covering. | Test visual expectations/tactile associations through models or precise 3D composites. |
| **Space and interaction** | Ordinary rooms/streets become estranged immersive settings. | Alter scale/viewpoints physically; separate narrative animations from frequent controls digitally. |

---

## Practical Design Methods

1. **Write the desired sensory association before choosing an object**: Surprise should lead to theme-relevant smell, temperature, time, or other feelings rather than strangeness alone.
2. **Change one main dimension**: Favor scale, surface, or location while retaining ordinary references elsewhere; changing every baseline can destroy intelligibility.
3. **For realistic composites, unify perspective/light/contact**: Match light direction, shadow softness, and contact edges to intensify experiential absurdity through physical believability.
4. **Use poetry for images and certainty for operations**: Posters, publications, and galleries can support open meanings; buying, forms, and navigation need predictable feedback.

## Design Example

> **Contemporary translation (hypothetical): a sensory exhibition about the smell of a city after rain**
> - **Goal**: Evoke cross-sensory imagination of wet streets, soil, and air.
> - **Poster**: Keep an ordinary bus shelter, street perspective, lamps, and reflections realistic; transform only falling rain into a giant soft transparent ribbon passing through the shelter. This focused shape/material change evokes moist air wrapping the body.
> - **Space and website**: Turn the ribbon into a suspended guide through exhibition zones. Use real photographs to explain scent stations online, with direct standard ticket buttons; imagery inspires while information guides.

## Representative Cases and Application Boundaries

- **Representative case**: Meret Oppenheim's 1936 Paris [Object at MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/80997) covers cup, saucer, and spoon in gazelle fur. A precise mismatch between drinking and touch creates a powerful psychological/sensory effect without complex scenery.
- **Application boundaries**: Suitable for contemporary art/culture, experimental film, publishing, niche fragrance, and imaginative campaigns. Avoid confusing displacements in traffic warnings, documentary news, scientific evidence, and frequent-use tools. Never present surreal composites as documentary photographs.

---

## Related Styles and Common Misreadings

- **Related styles**:
  - **Surrealism vs [Pop Art](28-pop-art.md)**: Both use everyday things; Pop examines media reproduction, while Surrealism changes familiar relationships to evoke associations.
  - **Surrealism vs [Maximalism](30-maximalism.md)**: Density is unnecessary; one precisely displaced fur cup can carry the expression.
- **Common misreading**: Random combinations, oddness, or violated physics are sufficient. First establish the everyday reference, changed relationship, and visual connections. Depth of meaning and emotional effectiveness are later quality questions, not entry requirements.
