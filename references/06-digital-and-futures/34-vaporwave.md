# Vaporwave: Design Philosophy and Style Guide

- **English name**: Vaporwave
- **Concept type**: Internet-born musical/visual subculture and its design influence
- **Core definition**: Repetition, distortion, and temporal recombination of early digital interfaces, advertisements, and commercial-space memories bring nostalgia, virtual pleasure, and estranging distance together.

---

## Attribution Criteria

### 1. Scope and Claims
Separate three claims:
- **Vaporwave visual language**: In covers, posters, film, exhibitions, or websites, directly inspect old consumer/digital-memory materials and their looping, distortion, or temporal recombination. Musical samples and evidence of anti-consumerist politics are not prerequisites.
- **Vaporwave and neighboring musical microgenres**: To distinguish Vaporwave from hardvapour or related branches, listen to the audio structure and check production/community contexts. A cover cannot establish musical genre.
- **Sample provenance or political position**: Actual dates of borrowed materials and claims of anti-capitalist intention require interviews or production records.

### 2. Core Evidence Dimensions

| Core evidence dimension | How to observe or verify | Why it discriminates | Limits and alternative explanations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Old consumer images or early digital memories provide principal material** | Identify early operating-system windows, catalogs, department stores, low-resolution pixels, or TV-ad grammar. | Focuses on commercially/digitally mediated memories rather than ordinary antique displays. | Original drawings may simulate old media grammar. Distressed appearance does not prove a 1980s–1990s source date. |
| **Memory fragments are repeated, distorted, or juxtaposed across times** | Trace windows, products, and corridors looping, stretching, and leaving ordinary commercial contexts. | Distinguishes reprocessing from simply displaying old ads, web captures, or reconstructed interfaces. | Scan-line noise is optional; surreal collage, infinite loops, and empty stalled spaces can also create temporal displacement. |
| **Media traces and scenes establish temporal/psychological distance** | Check pixels, VHS discontinuities, compression, or repeated corridors acting on specific remembered subjects rather than a uniform old-photo filter. | Makes re-presented memory an observable structure instead of replacing evidence with nostalgia/emptiness impressions. | Emotional responses vary. Sweet pleasure or absence of melancholy does not exclude Vaporwave. |
| **Media reprocessing governs the image or experience** | Compare scenes, text/image relationships, and sequences for shared reconstruction logic. | Separates a primary orientation from a statue or pink-purple accent. | Visual work needs neither music nor Japanese nonsense text. Production specifications and rights are separate compliance/quality matters. |

- **Common supporting appearances**: Pink-purple-cyan gradients, classical plaster statues, kana/Chinese labels, perspective grids, VHS noise. Alone they are insufficient. A contemporary perfume ad with a gradient/statue but no old-media loops, distortion, or temporal reconstruction does not establish Vaporwave.

### 3. Combined Judgment and Neighboring Styles
1. **Judgment sequence**:
   - Identify the consumer/early-digital grammar, then its loops, scan distortion, or temporal displacement. If reprocessing governs, conclude **Primary style: Vaporwave visual language**. Color or one cropped statue alone is **Local borrowing** of symbols.
   - Unclear media/reconstruction means **Insufficient evidence**. No soundtrack is not a disqualification; visual form cannot establish musical microgenre or politics.
2. **Challenge neighboring explanations**:
   - Compare **[Y2K](33-y2k.md)**: Y2K models clear hardware, metal, and streamlined virtual forms; Vaporwave dismantles, loops, and distorts old media. Both may support **Mixed** visual language.
   - Compare **[Pop Art](../05-decoration-and-experimentation/28-pop-art.md)**: Both appropriate commercial images; inspect whether early digital/video temporal distance, stalled looping, and signal distortion govern. Mixed attribution is possible.
   - Compare **[Cyberpunk](31-cyberpunk.md)**: Technological systems/individual survival conflicts differ from media-memory reprocessing. Shared blue/purple/pink light does not merge them.

## Position and Background

Vaporwave took shape in early-2010s online music communities, album art, and memes, often drawing on 1980s–1990s TV ads, mall Muzak, early games, and older PC interfaces. [AIGA Eye on Design: Vaporwave is Dead, Long Live Vaporwave](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/vaporwave-is-dead-long-live-vaporwave/) discusses nostalgia and late-capitalist consumer culture. This is a useful cultural perspective, not a political declaration every creator must sign or a fixed visual formula.

---

## Design Philosophy

1. **Displace familiar media memories in time/space**: Extract old interfaces, malls, or ads and repeat, deform, or cross periods to make familiar but distant time experiences.
2. **Keep viewing distance within pleasant consumption imagery**: Soft digital colors, polished goods, and virtual leisure may comfort and feel empty simultaneously. The theme may be playful, warm, or austere.
3. **Turn technical traces into narrative**: Pixels, scan lines, tape wear, and copy errors can explain how memory was stored, compressed, and recirculated, rather than indiscriminate noise.

---

## Design Language

> Common visual features and contemporary suggestions do not define every musical branch or work.

| Design dimension | Typical characteristics | Contemporary application |
|---|---|---|
| **Composition and layout** | Temporal collage, empty virtual commercial/public spaces, stacked windows, corridors. | Organize around one media-memory theme and control transitions between eras. |
| **Color** | Sweet pink/cyan/purple, fluorescence, sun-fading, CRT shifts. | Set warm/cool tones by theme while maintaining text/background lightness contrast. |
| **Typography** | Early desktop bitmap type, serif italics, sale labels, multilingual consumer text. | Give every foreign phrase/label actual meaning and context; avoid meaningless kana/characters. |
| **Graphics and symbols** | Old windows, shops, statues, tropical plants, early 3D grids. | Build original or authorized thematic materials and retain source/transformation records. |
| **Materials and texture** | Pixels, interlaced scans, lossy artifacts, VHS traces. | Select one theme-relevant distortion locally and check information readability. |
| **Space and interaction** | Endless loops, stalled time, empty virtual malls/lobbies. | Use projection/sequences physically; real controls must not imitate crashes, frozen dialogs, or failure. |

---

## Practical Design Methods

1. **Choose the remembered medium/setting**: Define 1990s department-store catalogs, early desktops, or home tapes before selecting visual/audio clues.
2. **Apply one temporal-processing rule**: For example, infinite corridor repeats or local scan breaks/sun fading; build grammar rather than random period fragments.
3. **Balance old atmosphere and current communication**: Old windows/catalog layouts may frame imagery, but dates, venues, prices, and explanations must stay accurate/readable.
4. **Keep distortion in narrative layers**: Concentrate scans, misalignment, and loops in covers/transitions; navigation, playback, and reading remain stable and modern.

## Design Example

> **Contemporary translation (hypothetical): an evening oral-history exhibition about an old shopping street**
> - **Goal**: Present remembered commercial prosperity and the distance of looking back.
> - **Print and space**: Actual shop-window photographs and original retro catalog graphics repeat into a deep perspective corridor. Fading/scans affect backgrounds only; names, interview dates, and opening times remain high contrast. Slow projections can be paused/switched through physical buttons.
> - **Website**: A clear modern contents structure leads to interviews. Fake system errors and loading failures must not interrupt browsing as decorative tricks.

## Representative Cases and Application Boundaries

- **Representative cases**: Macintosh Plus's 2011 *Floral Shoppe* cover, discussed in [AIGA Eye on Design](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/vaporwave-is-dead-long-live-vaporwave/), juxtaposes early 3D grids, classical bust, pink, and Japanese consumer labels. [Dazed's hardvapour interviews](https://www.dazeddigital.com/music/article/30896/1/inside-harvapour-the-internet-s-latest-microgenre) show adjacent communities defining different tempos, textures, and attitudes; one stereotype cannot cover the full subculture.
- **Application boundaries**: Useful for independent music, youth publishing, experimental film, and exhibitions on digital memory, consumption, and media change. Avoid distortion/discontinuity/collage in documentary evidence, public safety, or tools needing reliable feedback, where credibility and operational certainty matter.

---

## Related Styles and Common Misreadings

- **Related styles**:
  - **Vaporwave vs [Y2K](33-y2k.md)**: Y2K uses streamlined 3D and clear gloss for digital-future devices; Vaporwave loops/distorts/reinterprets older commercial and digital memory. They often overlap today.
  - **Vaporwave vs [Cyberpunk](31-cyberpunk.md)**: High-tech surveillance/individual conflicts are a different cultural route; colored lighting is not a shared identity.
- **Common misreading**: A formula of pink-purple, a David bust, and Japanese noise. Explain which collective memory a medium evokes and why it is repeated/distorted before organizing materials.
