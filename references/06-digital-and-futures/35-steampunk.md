# Steampunk: Design Philosophy and Style Guide

- **English name**: Steampunk
- **Concept type**: Science-fiction, craft, and visual culture around alternative technology and historical industry
- **Core definition**: Nineteenth-century steam machinery and craft traditions are reworked into an unrealized alternate world with perceptible mechanical logic and material detail.

---

## Attribution Criteria

### 1. Scope and Claims
Separate three claims:
- **Visual/world-building borrowing**: For props, dress, illustration, exhibitions, or interfaces, inspect how steam-era mechanical/instrument vocabulary becomes fictional uses, character equipment, or alternative living systems. A reference list and actual steam-powered operation are unnecessary.
- **Literary/film genre**: Read the alternate-history background and plot conflicts; a brass prop photo cannot classify an entire narrative.
- **Authentic artifacts or hand manufacture**: Nineteenth-century instrument identity, hand forging, or actual mechanical function requires historical, production, or engineering evidence rather than appearance.

### 2. Core Evidence Dimensions

| Core evidence dimension | How to observe or verify | Why it discriminates | Limits and alternative explanations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Steam-era machinery, instruments, or clothing provide main references** | Map valves, pressure pipes, gauges, rods, optical tubes, and plates in combination rather than simply brass color. | Anchors imagination in recognizable nineteenth-century industry/science rather than generic electronic glow. | Fixed parts or Victorian formal dress are not mandatory. A single gear/brass piece may be an ordinary antique or ornament. |
| **Historical machinery is reconstructed into fictional use, equipment, or systems** | Identify reimagined connections, functions, wearable proportions, or cross-domain assemblies such as mechanical wings or an ether weather instrument. | Distinguishes alternate-world construction from unchanged instruments and ordinary industrial interiors. | Clothing relationships, diagrams, or scenes may imply fiction without a novel. Unknown actual uses should remain unverified rather than assumed fictional. |
| **Parts have recognizable visual/narrative roles** | Trace collection, transmission/recording, display, handles, and bodily attachments. | Establishes an imagined device/world logic rather than unrelated glued old parts. | Roles can be fictional, comic, or absurd; gears need not mesh under load and valves need not carry steam. |
| **Fictional machinery relationships govern the object** | Compare contours, core parts, labels, and setting for shared alternate technical logic. | Separates a core setting from a gear pendant on an ordinary product. | Real brass, handwork, and precision are unnecessary. Painted-plastic rough models may qualify; craftsmanship and engineering safety are separate. |

- **Common supporting appearances**: Brass paint, gears, goggles, leather straps, patina. Alone these are insufficient. A documented unchanged nineteenth-century brass clock is an artifact, not automatically Steampunk because it has gears and age.

### 3. Combined Judgment and Neighboring Styles
1. **Judgment sequence**:
   - Confirm steam-era/scientific vocabulary, then fictional devices, wearables, or alternative life through component roles. If dominant, conclude **Primary style: Steampunk visual/world-building language**. Scattered glued gears are **Local borrowing** of symbols.
   - A cropped gauge that could be antique or fictional requires **Insufficient evidence**, with full structure/context requested.
2. **Challenge neighboring explanations**:
   - Compare **[Industrial](../04-materials-and-everyday-life/22-industrial.md)**: Industrial interiors draw on practical factory framing, services, concrete, and workshop furniture in everyday space; Steampunk builds fictional alternate worlds from historical machinery.
   - Compare **[Retrofuturism](32-retrofuturism.md)**: Retrofuturism revisits broader periods, including Space/Atomic Ages; Steampunk concentrates on steam-era alternate technology. They can overlap or form subset relationships.

### 4. Worked Attribution Examples

| Example type | Input facts → conclusion | Evidence and outstanding questions |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive example (hypothetical)** | A labeled fictional mechanical-weather backpack joins a wind funnel, spring recording wheel, and chest-mounted brass pressure display through pipes, combining optical instruments and wearable frame; diagrams explain the weather observer → **Primary style: Steampunk, jointly organized by historical instruments and fictional function**. | **Evidence**: Collection, recording, and display have clear fictional roles. **Outstanding**: Nonturning gears and painted plastic do not cancel attribution; they affect fabrication assessment. |
| **Counterexample (hypothetical)** | A museum displays a late-nineteenth-century brass clock with original casing/mechanism and a dated maker label, without alternative modification → **Attribution not supported as Steampunk; authentic historical machinery**. | **Evidence**: Brass/gears/history lack alternate-world reconstruction. **Outstanding**: No further test is needed to separate artifact identity from the modern fiction label. |
| **Boundary example (hypothetical)** | A factory-conversion cafe retains cast-iron pipes, brick, and concrete, adding one poster of a steam aircraft and fictional pressure labels → **The poster borrows Steampunk; the main cafe remains Industrial**. | **Evidence**: Actual industrial framing governs the space; one fictional image cannot classify the whole. **Outstanding**: A crop showing only one gauge could leave even the poster's fiction unverified. |

## Position and Background

Steampunk combines Victorian-era technological associations with contemporary fantasy/science fiction across installations, props, costumes, publishing, and craft. Oxford's Museum of the History of Science [Broad Sheet No. 9](https://www.mhs.ox.ac.uk/exhibits/steampunk/wp-content/uploads/broadsheet9.pdf) describes historical/contemporary and mechanical/artistic encounters. Working devices and purely narrative concepts coexist; neither should be confused with actual nineteenth-century scientific artifacts.

---

## Design Philosophy

1. **Establish coherent alternative technical rules**: Imagine energy sources, transmission, and control so pipes/gauges appear to grow from understandable rules.
2. **Make joints and making traces narrative**: Rivets, etched scales, forging, and material joins can show craft/industry encounters and give details structural roles.
3. **Let details suggest character and world**: Portability, protection, and repairs can imply occupation, class, and exploration; complexity should grow from those stories.

---

## Design Language

> These contemporary suggestions are not historical engineering rules or authentic Victorian dress requirements.

| Design dimension | Typical characteristics | Contemporary application |
|---|---|---|
| **Composition and layout** | Instrument panels, exploded parts, natural-history plates, workshop display. | Organize parts/modules by fictional power–transmission–display logic. |
| **Color** | Brass, copper, walnut, optical glass, brown leather, oxidation/oil associations. | Derive colors from chosen materials/processes rather than uniform dirt filters. |
| **Typography** | Etched plates, dial numerals, patents, letterpress newspaper titles. | Establish readable device-name/parameter/instruction hierarchies and accessible long text. |
| **Graphics and symbols** | Gears, valves, dials, springs, airships, workshop marks. | Assign fictional roles and distinguish background ornaments from functional feedback icons. |
| **Materials and texture** | Brass, iron, wood, blown glass, leather, old/new assemblies. | Fit bolts/finishes to actual production and safety while retaining tactile contrasts. |
| **Space and interaction** | Watchmakers, laboratories, airship cabins, explorers' equipment rooms. | Tell character stories through objects; borrow panel layers online without turning basic tasks into mechanical puzzles. |

---

## Practical Design Methods

1. **Write a working story and map energy/information**: State what a device collects, what drives it, and how results reach users before shaping cases/accessories.
2. **Separate working, narrative, and decorative parts**: Identify actual structural/interactive components versus world-building props so people do not overestimate engineering capabilities.
3. **Unify plates under one fictional institution**: A nineteenth-century royal science society or invented workshop can share numbering, inspection marks, scales, and diagrams.
4. **Ensure safety and direct operation**: Test stability/edges physically. Label digital controls in plain current language; users need not learn fictional barometers to click.

## Design Example

> **Contemporary translation (hypothetical): a fictional mechanical weather instrument for an urban science-imagination week**
> - **Goal**: Ask how city life would differ if nineteenth-century weather reporting relied entirely on machines.
> - **Prop and print**: Use three clear parts: wind/humidity funnel, spring-gear recording wheel, and brass flip display. A plate states contemporary art concept model. Patent-like orthographic posters explain the imagined working; tickets reuse workshop IDs/inspection emblems.
> - **Website**: A playful mechanical-weather story generator borrows brass-panel framing. Date filters, reservations, and switches keep plain modern labels so visitors need no mechanical training.

## Representative Cases and Application Boundaries

- **Representative cases**: Oxford's [Steampunk exhibition archive (2009–2010)](https://www.mhs.ox.ac.uk/exhibits/steampunk/) documents intersections of art, object design, and craft. [Broad Sheet No. 9](https://www.mhs.ox.ac.uk/exhibits/steampunk/wp-content/uploads/broadsheet9.pdf) names Art Donovan's *Siddhartha Pod* and *Thin White Duke* lighting, combining brass, wood, and glass into imagined scientific forms. These do not restrict all Steampunk to lamps.
- **Application boundaries**: Useful for theater/film props, immersive exhibitions, craft art, tabletop games, and narrative cultural brands. Science-history education and engineering product communications must distinguish authentic artifacts/real principles from contemporary fictional settings, avoiding misleading scientific or performance claims.

---

## Related Styles and Common Misreadings

- **Related styles**:
  - **Steampunk vs [Retrofuturism](32-retrofuturism.md)**: Retrofuturism revisits many eras; steam-era alternate worlds form a particular overlapping branch.
  - **Steampunk vs [Industrial](../04-materials-and-everyday-life/22-industrial.md)**: Practical factory/material reuse differs from fictional world-building; exposed metal pipes/rivets alone do not merge them.
- **Common misreading**: Painting an object bronze and gluing unrelated plastic gears produces complete Steampunk. Classification examines historical machinery/fictional-function relationships; authentic materials, workmanship, and actual motion require separate craft/engineering evaluation.
